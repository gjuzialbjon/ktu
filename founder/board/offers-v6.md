# Offers lens (round 6)
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF
Unchanged from round 5. Narrowing the app to Tirana and making the attendee side easier to use help a little. But the Offers lens scores the paying market first, and no market evidence, prices or guarantees have been added. A better demo still leaves the buyer's likelihood score where it was.

## What is strong

- **The attendee side's value equation got better on effort and likelihood.** A denser home screen, filters shown as removable chips, and "never show me" topics all cut the effort of finding something worth going to. The event page tells the attendee what to expect before they go: duration, price, spots left, languages, weather and a step-by-step programme. That raises how sure they feel the evening will be good. The "Coming alone? That's fine" collection speaks straight to the stranger-anxiety objection.
- **Scope is narrower.** Only Tirana is live, and the other cities sit behind a flag. One market, one feed: the offer is easier to deliver and to judge.
- **Language tags could make a niche offer.** For visitors and newcomers, "events that work in English or Italian" is a specific outcome that nobody in the competitor map is shown offering. It is untested, and the demo tags were set by the founders.
- **Honest scarcity still holds:** at most 2 labeled sponsored slots, "10 founding partners", and event dates that are real deadlines.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No paying buyer has been asked anything, for the sixth round running.** Promoter spend, willingness to pay for placement or commission, and partner interest are all still unknown. At about €7 a ticket, each 1% of commission is about €0.07, so placement has to carry early revenue, and placement has no price. The commercial side has no price, no guarantee and no buyer. On this lens that is the deciding gap.
2. **The stamp card is still in the app with no answer on who pays for it.** "5 partners → 20% off" still has no funder. If the platform pays, every engaged user costs money. If partners pay, they are asked for a fee plus discounts before seeing any foot traffic. Shipping it makes it look like a promise to partners that nobody has priced.
3. **The offline delivery is promised on screen but not real.** The programme waypoints and named "host" tell attendees what will happen. No host is recruited or paid, and there is no safety, reporting or no-show rule. If a stranger dinner falls flat, the platform takes the blame. The likelihood score depends on this, and its cost is unknown.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What will a mid-tier promoter pay for one sponsored slot, and how many extra tickets must it sell to be worth that? (/founder-consumer on organizers, /founder-pricing)
- Who funds the stamp-card discount, and does it make or lose money per redemption under each option? (/founder-cfo)
- What does a reliable host cost per event, and how many attendees are needed to break even? (/founder-cfo after a hand-run test)
- How many Tirana events really run in English, Italian or Spanish, and would visitors find the app? (/founder-competitors, /founder-consumer on visitors)
- What guarantee can you offer a paying promoter or partner (for example, no measured clicks or foot traffic means no fee), and can you afford it? (/founder-pricing, /founder-cfo)
- Could you charge double for placement and still be the obvious choice over Instagram ads? Nobody can answer that without asking buyers.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Stop new app features** until conditions 2–4 are met. Move the Perks tab and stamp card behind the same flag as the other cities.
2. **Hold 15–20 promoter/venue interviews and 10 or more partner interviews**, using the demo as the sales tool. They must establish current spend, willingness to pay and who funds discounts.
3. **Write one priced, one-page offer per buyer type, each with a guarantee the numbers can afford.** Set a signed-commitment threshold in advance, and stop or pivot if it is missed.
4. **Run the stage-1 test and one hand-run hosted event**, with targets written in advance (attendance, no-show rate, week-two return), a known cost per host and a basic safety/no-show policy.
