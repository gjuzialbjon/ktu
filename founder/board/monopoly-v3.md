# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **It starts with a small market, on purpose.** The Monopoly lens wants a big share of a small market, then expansion — and "all events in Tirana" is exactly that shape: one city, a bounded set of organizers (described as hundreds), expansion to other Albanian cities only after dominance. This is the right geometry; most briefs get it wrong.
- **There is a candidate secret.** The claim that small free community events are served by nobody — not Instagram, not the three local platforms — is a genuine "what do you know that others don't" answer. If true, it means the incumbents are all fighting over the commercial segment while the audience-building segment sits empty. The staged rollout is designed to test the secret cheaply before anyone writes code, which the lens respects: distribution first, product second.
- **A real network-effect story, if liquidity arrives.** Two-sided and self-reinforcing: community events create daily attention, attention is the only thing commercial organizers actually want to buy, and a genuinely *complete* feed is the one thing a fragmented Instagram ecosystem cannot offer. Completeness in a single city is winner-take-most: once one feed has everything, a second complete feed is pointless. That is the only durable moat available to this business, and the plan at least aims at it.
- **Distribution is answered, not hand-waved.** Stage 1 (curated Instagram/WhatsApp feed, zero code) is a concrete answer to "how do the first 100 users arrive and what does each cost" — approximately zero cash, founder labor. The pre-commitment gate on the commercial side ("signed commitments or stop") answers how the first paying customers arrive before the product exists.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The people question fails as briefed.** Three part-time founders; two live in Barcelona and London; the one business whose entire moat is hyper-local liquidity — personally seeding yoga sessions, attending chess tournaments, hand-onboarding organizers — has at most one founder sometimes in Tirana, and nobody has decided to go full-time. The lens asks "is this the right team?" and the honest answer from the brief is: not in its current configuration. A liquidity business run part-time from three countries loses to the first full-time local who copies it. This is the single most likely cause of death, and it is entirely inside the founders' control.
2. **Nothing stops a copycat in month six — except speed the team cannot currently supply.** There is no proprietary technology (a feed, RSVPs and ticketing are weeks of commodity engineering), no brand yet, no scale economies at this size. The only moat is network effects, which do not exist until liquidity does. The curated-feed stage is trivially imitable by any of the three incumbents (Kuadr, Enter Event, Go Wild) or by a venue group with an intern; the brief itself admits nobody knows why those incumbents stalled. If they stalled because the market can't support them, this dies with them; if they stalled from lack of effort, they are one competent hire away from waking up. Open question, named in the brief: why did the incumbents stay small, and what do promoters currently spend — /founder-competitors must answer before any money is committed.
3. **The monetizable market may be too small to matter even if monopoly is achieved.** The lens prizes dominating a small market, but the market must be worth dominating. Revenue is a commission on an unknown number of ticketed events at unknown prices, plus sponsorship from an unknown promotion spend, in a country with low disposable income. "Unknown but nonzero" is the brief's own phrase. It is possible to win 100% of Tirana's event-attention market and still have revenue that never supports one full-time salary, let alone three. Nobody has these numbers: ticketed-event volume and promoter spend → /founder-competitors; commission rate and sponsorship pricing → /founder-pricing; whether the resulting monopoly covers its own costs → /founder-cfo. Related: whether commercial organizers will route ticket sales through a new platform at all → /founder-consumer run on organizers, not attendees.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. Which of you goes full-time, and in what month? If the answer is "none until revenue," explain how a part-time remote team out-executes a full-time local copycat in a business whose moat is physical presence.
2. Why did Kuadr, Enter Event and Go Wild stay small? Give me their history, not a guess. (→ /founder-competitors)
3. What is the total annual ticketing volume and promotion spend in Tirana — the ceiling of your revenue even at 100% share? (→ /founder-competitors for the market facts, /founder-cfo for what that ceiling must be for this to be a business)
4. What exactly is your secret that survives being seen? The moment the curated feed works, the idea is public. What compounds for you between month one and month six that a copier cannot buy?
5. What are the numeric gates? "Follower growth" and "a stated minimum of commitments" are placeholders. What number at stage 1 and stage 3 means stop?
6. Will promoters actually move ticket sales onto a new platform, or only buy placement while keeping ticketing where it is? (→ /founder-consumer on organizers; it changes which of your two revenue lines is real)
7. In ten years, is this still the event layer of Albanian cities, or a feature Instagram's local-events surface absorbed? What makes it durable beyond "we were first"?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

Funding here means time and small money for stages 1–3 only; nothing for the app until the gates clear.

1. **One founder commits full-time and in Tirana** — named, dated, before stage 2 begins. Without this the vote is PASS regardless of the other conditions.
2. **Numeric gates in writing before stage 1 starts:** a follower target, a verified follower-to-attendee conversion rate, and a minimum count and value of signed commercial commitments at stage 3 — each with a stop decision attached, agreed now, not after the results arrive.
3. **The competitor question answered with evidence:** /founder-competitors delivers why the three incumbents stalled, what they charge, and Tirana's actual ticketed-event volume and promotion spend. If the ceiling cannot plausibly fund the business at full monopoly, stop before the MVP.
4. **Organizer willingness tested, not assumed:** /founder-consumer run on commercial organizers for ticketing-migration intent, and stage-3 commitments signed at a real commission rate and a real placement price (→ /founder-pricing), not letters of goodwill.
5. **The cash case re-derived:** /founder-cfo rebuilds break-even from the real rates in condition 4 before any build spend is approved.
