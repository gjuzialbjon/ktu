# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The experience the founders describe is real and vivid.** "A yoga session by the lake for 8 people" is a concrete moment, and it is not what Instagram, Facebook or the local listing platforms (Kuadr Events, Enter Event, Go Wild) deliver. The Product lens says to start from the moment of use and work backwards; the founders have at least found a moment worth building towards: an ordinary person creating a small event and strangers actually turning up.
- **The one-city constraint is a gift.** Tirana-only is the kind of forced focus this lens prizes. A small, dense, walkable city with a fragmented event scene means the team can hand-craft the experience — personally onboarding the first 10–15 organizers, attending the first events, fixing seams in person. That is how a product with taste gets built: at a scale where the founders can see every rough edge.
- **The problem statement is simple.** "You can't find out what's happening in Tirana in one place" fits in a sentence. The pitch for the problem passes the simplicity test, even if the pitch for the product (see below) does not.
- **The team can build it.** Two experienced engineers and a PM/designer is the right shape for a product this size — provided they ever get the hours (a team question, not a product one, so it does not move the score).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **Six products pretending to be one, built in part-time hours.** Creation, discovery, RSVP, real-time notifications, geolocation/maps, ratings and comments, image uploads, an admin panel — this is a feature list, not a product. The Product lens says focus means saying no, and nothing here has been said no to. The founders themselves rate the technology risk HIGH and already hint at the cut (ratings deferred to v2, minimal MVP of create/discover/RSVP) — but "minimal" still spans three functions and two sides of a marketplace, on nights and weekends. The most likely death: eighteen months of part-time work produces a mediocre version of everything and a brilliant version of nothing, and users go back to Instagram, which is mediocre but already open on their phone.
2. **The first five minutes are empty.** Describe the moment of first use: a user in Tirana downloads the app, opens discovery, and sees — what? With no seeded content, the honest answer is three events, none near them, none in their interests. For an organizer: they post an event and nobody RSVPs. The founders name the chicken-and-egg as a market risk, but through this lens it is an experience defect in the product's most important moment, and nothing in the plan designs around it. "10–15 partner organizers" is an answer to supply on paper, not to what the screen actually shows on day one. An empty-feeling first session is a deleted app; in a city of 500,000 the pool of second chances is small.
3. **The experience is handed off at every seam, and the seams are not designed.** The product's promise ends with a real-world moment the app does not control: eight strangers showing up at the lake. What happens when the organizer cancels an hour before? When nobody comes? When the event is bad or unsafe? The rating system — the one trust mechanism, the thing informal interviewees called "very useful" — is deferred to v2. The lens says every touchpoint is the product and seams are where it breaks; here the entire payoff of the product happens offline, after the last screen, with no plan for when it goes wrong. One widely-shared bad experience ("I went and no one was there") poisons a small city fast.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. **What is the one thing?** If you could only ship one of creation, discovery, RSVP, notifications, or ratings — which one, and why would someone open the app for that alone? "A complete, centralized solution" is a paragraph, not a sentence.
2. **Walk me through minute one through five** for a user who downloads the app in week two, before you have content. What exactly is on the screen? And the same for the first organizer who posts an event that gets zero RSVPs.
3. **What have you cut, and what will you refuse to build?** The brief lists what is in; a product plan is defined by what is out. Would you ship without maps? Without native apps (a web app or even a curated Instagram/WhatsApp concierge for the first 90 days)?
4. **Who owns the quality bar?** Three part-timers in three countries and two time zones — when a detail is wrong at 11pm before launch, whose taste decides, and who has the hours to act on it?
5. **What happens after the RSVP?** The product's actual outcome is a stranger walking into a room. How do you design for no-shows, cancellations, and the first bad event, given ratings are deferred?
6. Open questions this lens cannot settle and the pack should: whether users would actually switch from Instagram and what the first session must contain to keep them (**/founder-consumer**); what Kuadr Events, Enter Event and Go Wild actually do well and poorly in their users' own words, i.e. the experience gap to own (**/founder-competitors**); and whether the 6–9 month revenue gap plus break-even at 100–150 paying clients is survivable on the unnamed budget (**/founder-cfo** — no budget, CAC or pricing figures exist in the brief).

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **One sentence, one thing.** Before any code: a single-sentence product definition the team can say no with, and a v1 cut to the smallest loop that proves the bet — plausibly: browse a full, curated Tirana event feed and RSVP in one tap. Creation-by-anyone, ratings, and real-time anything come only after that loop retains users.
2. **Solve day-one emptiness before building the app.** Run a content-first test with no custom software — a hand-curated weekly feed of every Tirana event (a channel, a simple page) for 4–6 weeks. If the founders cannot get people to follow a free, complete, curated feed, no app features will save it. This doubles as the structured demand validation the brief admits is missing.
3. **Design the offline seam.** A written plan for cancellations, no-shows, and bad events in v1 — even if it is manual (the founders personally monitoring every event in the first months). Trust is the product's core claim; it cannot wait for v2.
4. **A validated first-session spec.** Results from /founder-consumer on what a first-time Tirana user needs to see to return in week two, before the MVP is scoped.
5. **Hours, in writing.** A stated weekly hour commitment per founder and a single decision-maker on product quality. Part-time is survivable only if the scope in condition 1 is honored; the two conditions stand or fall together.
