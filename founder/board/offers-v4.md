# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The staged rollout is, in effect, offer-testing before product-building.** Stage 3 — signed commission terms and placement prices from commercial organizers before any code is written — is exactly what the Offers lens wants: proof that a named buyer with money will say yes to a concrete offer, gated with a stop condition. Few briefs have this discipline, and stage 1 costs nothing to run.
- **The audience-first sequencing attacks the likelihood problem head-on.** The value equation says offers mostly fail on perceived likelihood (no proof). Selling reach only after the free side has demonstrated reach means that when the commercial offer is finally made, it carries its own evidence — follower counts and verified follower-to-attendee conversion, not promises.
- **The verified landscape removes the "failed market" ghost.** Kuadr, Enter Events and Go Wild never attempted general discovery; the community slot (yoga, chess, book clubs) is served by nobody, and the only occupant of the discovery slot (Even8) is thin — ~37 events, no visible business model. For the free layer, "everything happening in Tirana, one tap" against WhatsApp fragmentation is a clear more-outcome-less-effort offer.
- **Real urgency and scarcity levers exist.** Top-of-feed sponsored placement is genuinely scarce (one feed, limited slots), and event dates create honest deadlines. The stack does not need to invent fake urgency.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The paying market may fail the market test — and a weak market is the most common reason this lens passes.** The lens scores a market on massive pain, money to spend, reachability and growth. The actual buyer here is not the 500,000 residents; it is the mid-tier Tirana promoter. Their money is unverified (promoter promotion spend is explicitly unknown), typical tickets are ~€7 so per-ticket commission is small, only 8.6% of Albanians bought event tickets online in the Eurostat window, and nobody publishes fees because pricing is relationship-based — a signal that deals are won over coffee, not through a self-serve offer. The brief itself concedes commission may not carry the model. If the buyer pool is a few dozen mid-tier promoters each worth a small commission stream, no offer construction fixes that.
2. **The commercial offer asks for enormous effort and sacrifice from the buyer.** Routing ticket sales through a new platform means abandoning cash/agent channels (EasyPay's nationwide network) and in-house systems in a market where most tickets still move through cash. The value equation punishes effort and sacrifice directly: a promoter adopting this gives up a working channel for an unproven one. The fallback — sponsored placement alone — is a weaker offer that competes with simply buying Instagram ads, which the promoter already knows how to do. Right now there is no answer to "what makes a promoter feel stupid saying no?"
3. **The chicken-and-egg sits exactly at the proof step.** Stage 3 asks organizers to sign commission terms before the app exists, when the only evidence is Instagram followers and hand-run events. If reach proof arrives slowly — or Even8, EasyPay or a promoter's own Instagram following is "good enough" — the pre-commit gate fails, and under the brief's own rules the business stops. The free layer builds an audience, but an audience of free-event joiners (book clubs, hikes) is not automatically the ticket-buying audience a concert promoter pays for; that overlap is asserted, not shown.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- **What is the dream outcome for a mid-tier promoter, in numbers?** "Reach the audience" is not an offer. How many incremental tickets does placement or integrated ticketing sell for them, versus their current Instagram post? Nobody knows this today — it needs organizer interviews (/founder-consumer run on organizers, not attendees).
- **What will a promoter actually pay, and in what structure?** Commission rate and placement price are both unset; incumbent fees are unpublished. What is the price range where this is a bargain and where it dies? (/founder-pricing — the Van Westendorp pass must include organizers, not just ticket buyers.)
- **How many paying organizers at what revenue each does the model need to matter?** At ~€7 tickets and single-digit commission, is the whole Tirana commission pool even worth operating for, or must placement carry it? (/founder-cfo, after /founder-pricing supplies real rates.)
- **What is the guarantee?** The lens wants risk reversal. "No tickets sold through us this month, placement is free" is the obvious candidate — can the economics afford it? (/founder-cfo.)
- **What exactly is the stage-3 "stated minimum of hard commitments"?** A gate with an unset threshold is not a gate. Name the number and the signature standard before stage 1 starts.
- **Could you double the placement price and still be the obvious choice over an Instagram ad?** If the honest answer is no, the offer is a commodity and the lens says do not build it on price.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Stage 1 passes with a hard, pre-registered bar:** follower growth and a measured follower-to-attendee conversion rate, numbers written down before the feed launches, not after.
2. **Organizer evidence before any build:** structured interviews with at least 15–20 mid-tier promoters/venues (/founder-consumer on organizers) establishing their current promotion spend, their pain in their own words, and their stated willingness to pay — closing the three "not publicly findable" gaps the brief names.
3. **A priced, guaranteed commercial offer exists on paper:** commission rate, placement price (/founder-pricing) and a risk-reversal guarantee the unit economics can afford (/founder-cfo), expressed as a one-page offer a promoter can sign.
4. **Stage 3 clears with a named threshold:** a pre-declared minimum number of signed commitments at those real prices — and the founders honour their own stop rule if it misses.
5. **Evidence that the free audience converts to the paying audience:** at least one hand-run commercial event where the community feed demonstrably sold tickets, before the MVP is funded.
