# Offers lens (round 5)
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

Score unchanged from round 4. The prototype makes the attendee offer easier to show, but the Offers lens scores the market first, and the brief has no new market evidence. A demo cannot raise the likelihood score for a buyer who has never been asked. The partner revenue idea adds a buyer that may be easier to reach, which helps a little. Building before any demand was tested, and adding seven cities, costs about as much as that gains.

## What is strong

- **The attendee offer now has a better value equation.** Joining is one tap, ticketed events are reserved in the app and paid in cash at the door with a QR code, and "your crew" shows which friends are going. That last point answers the panel's top condition for attending (seeing people they know going). Effort is low and likelihood is visible. The cash-at-door choice fits a market where only 8.6% bought tickets online.
- **Partner offers are real stack thinking.** A discount attached to the event ("10% off the book club pick", 5% off dinner for the table) adds value for the attendee at little cost to the platform, as long as the partner funds it. It also creates a third buyer, local cafés and shops. They may be easier to find and sell to face to face than promoters, but that is untested.
- **A demo is a sales asset.** When the organizer and partner conversations happen, a working app makes the commercial offer more believable than slides would.
- **Honest scarcity still holds:** at most 2 labeled sponsored slots per city feed, "10 founding partners", and event dates that create real deadlines.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The paying market is still untested, and that is the main thing this lens checks.** Promoter spend, willingness to pay for placement or commission, and partner willingness to join are all unknown. Typical tickets are about €7, so each 1% of commission is about €0.07 a ticket. The new partner idea comes with no pricing, no buyer and no answer to who pays the discount. Three possible revenue streams with no evidence behind any of them is not proof.
2. **The stamp card could lose money on every redemption.** "5 partners, get 20% off" is undecided on both who funds the 20% and what partners pay. If the platform pays it, every engaged user costs money. If partners pay, each one is asked to give discounts plus a fee before seeing any foot traffic, which is a lot of effort and sacrifice for an unproven return.
3. **The offer is spread too thin.** Eight cities with no research outside Tirana, platform-run events that need a supply of reliable hosts whose cost is unknown, and meet-ups with strangers with no safety or trust design. Each of these adds delivery cost and risk to the likelihood score before the core offer in one city has worked once.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What will a café or restaurant pay to be a partner, and who funds the discount? Interview real partners, then price it (/founder-consumer on partners, then /founder-pricing).
- Does the stamp card make money per redemption under each funding model? (/founder-cfo)
- What is a mid-tier promoter's current promotion spend, and how many extra tickets would placement have to sell to be worth paying for? (/founder-consumer on organizers, /founder-competitors on what Instagram ads and EasyPay cost them)
- What does a reliable host cost per platform-run event, and do strangers actually turn up? (/founder-cfo after a hand-run test)
- Why eight cities now? What evidence says the offer works outside Tirana?
- What is the guarantee for a paying partner or promoter (for example, no measurable foot traffic means no fee) and can the business afford it? (/founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Freeze scope to Tirana** and the core feed until the rest of these conditions are met. The other seven cities and the stamp card stay in the demo only.
2. **Run stage 1 with targets written down in advance.** Follower growth, the share of followers who actually attend, and week-two retention, each with the target set before launch.
3. **Get commercial evidence before any backend or payments work.** Run 15–20 structured interviews with promoters and venues, plus 10 or more with prospective partners, using the prototype as the sales demo. They must establish current spend, willingness to pay, and who funds discounts.
4. **Write a one-page priced offer for each buyer type** (promoter and partner), with a guarantee the unit economics can afford (/founder-pricing, /founder-cfo). Get signed commitments up to a pre-stated threshold, and stop if the threshold is missed.
5. **Prove that hand-run platform events work** at a known cost per host, with a basic safety and no-show policy, before the system generates events automatically.
