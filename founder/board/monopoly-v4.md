# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The small-market instinct is exactly what the Monopoly lens asks for.** One city, one audience, dominate before expanding. Tirana (500k+, young, high smartphone use) is a definable market a tiny team could plausibly own, and "other Albanian cities only after Tirana works" is the right sequencing.
- **The slot is verified empty-ish, and the absence of a failed predecessor is now a fact, not a hope.** The v4 research shows no general-discovery platform ever tried and died here; the supposed incumbents (Kuadr, Enter Events, Go Wild) are all nightlife/commercial players who never attempted this product. The one occupant, Even8, is thin (~37 events, "29+ community members", no visible business model). A big share of a small market is actually available to be taken.
- **The free community layer is a credible path to a network effect** — the only durable moat on offer here. Yoga, chess and book clubs are served by nobody; if the platform becomes where small events live, each organizer brings attendees who become organizers, and the commercial side then pays for access to an audience nobody else has assembled. That is the correct Monopoly-lens architecture: subsidized side builds the network, concentrated side monetizes it.
- **Distribution-first staging.** The lens says a product with no way to reach customers is not a business; this plan starts with distribution (a curated Instagram/WhatsApp feed at zero cost) and refuses to build until follower-to-attendee conversion and pre-committed commercial terms exist. Stage 3 — signed commission terms before any code — is unusually honest gating.
- **A plausible secret.** "Tirana's social life is fragmented across Instagram and word of mouth, nobody has aggregated it, and the incumbents are structurally uninterested because they only monetize the nightlife slice" is a real, verified, non-obvious claim about the market.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The monetizable market may be too small to be worth monopolizing.** A monopoly over a market with no profit in it is worthless, and the verified numbers cut against the revenue layer: 8.6% online-ticketing penetration, typical local tickets at ~€7 (so commission per ticket is cents-to-a-euro), big promoters and festivals keeping ticketing in-house, EasyPay owning the online slice with a nationwide cash-agent network, and all organizer pricing relationship-based and unpublished. The founders already concede sponsorship may have to carry the model — but placement is priced at zero data too. The lens asks "who has money to spend here?" and the brief cannot yet answer. The demand spikes (Kanye, 10,000 tickets in 2 hours) belong to exactly the large events this platform will never ticket.
2. **A part-time, mostly absent team in a relationship-priced market.** Two of three founders live in Barcelona and London; the one in Tirana splits time with Milan; all three keep full-time jobs; nobody has committed to going full-time. The commercial layer runs on relationships ("nobody publishes fees — pricing is relationship-based"), which means in-person organizer sales in Tirana, week after week. The lens's people question fails as briefed: this is a ground game being planned from three cities, none of them reliably Tirana. Hand-curating a complete weekly feed and hand-onboarding organizers is itself a part-time-killing workload.
3. **Nothing stops a copycat in month six — the moat arrives only after liquidity, and liquidity is unproven.** A curated feed is copyable by anyone with an Instagram account; the MVP (browse, join, buy) is commodity software. Even8 is already live in the slot and only needs to execute; EasyPay or Enter Events could bolt discovery onto existing ticketing relationships in a quarter; Instagram itself remains the free, entrenched substitute the whole city already uses. Until the community network effect demonstrably forms (people joining because other people are there, not because the feed is nice), this business has none of the four durability sources — no proprietary tech, no scale economics, no brand, and a network effect that exists only on the slide.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What is the secret Even8 is missing? They are live, in your slot, with search/save/join and gamification. Why do you win and they stay thin — and what do you do the month they raise money or copy your feed?
- Which founder goes full-time, in Tirana, and on what date? If the answer is "undecided," why should the board believe the relationship-sales stage can happen at all?
- What does the commercial layer actually earn per event? Commission rate, placement price, and mid-tier promoter promotion spend are all unset or unknown. Open question — no public source exists; it needs organizer interviews via /founder-consumer (run on organizers) and /founder-pricing before any revenue claim is admissible.
- What are the stage gates, numerically? "Follower growth," "verified conversion," and "a stated minimum of hard commitments" have no numbers attached. What follower-to-attendee rate kills the project? How many signed commercial terms is "stop"?
- If commission is structurally tiny (€7 tickets, 8.6% online penetration), what is the ten-year business — a media/placement business, a payments business, or an eventual ticketing rail as online penetration grows? Which one are you building toward, and what does break-even look like under each? Open question — /founder-cfo, after /founder-pricing supplies real rates.
- Why now? What changed in Tirana in 2026 that makes this work when nobody has tried it — rising online-payment adoption, diaspora return, event volume? Name the trend and the evidence for it.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

Fund nothing but founder time until all four hold:

1. **Stage 1 passes a pre-registered numeric gate.** Before week 1, write down the follower count and the follower-to-attendee conversion rate that count as success, and the numbers that mean stop. The gate exists in the plan; it must exist as numbers.
2. **One named founder commits full-time in Tirana** before any code is written. Without this, the relationship-priced commercial layer and the hand-onboarding of community organizers are not executable, and the board should treat the plan as a side project, not a company.
3. **Stage 3 is honored literally:** a stated minimum (the founders set it in advance, in writing) of signed commission-and-placement terms from mid-tier organizers before the MVP is built — real rates on paper, replacing the "rate unset / price unset" blanks. /founder-pricing turns those terms into a price recommendation.
4. **The unit economics clear a bar at verified-plausible inputs.** With the Stage 3 rates and real organizer-interview data on promotion spend, /founder-cfo must show a credible path to covering at least one full-time Tirana salary from the commercial layer within a defined period. If €7 tickets and unpriced placement cannot support one person on the ground, the monopoly is over a market too small to matter, and the correct vote becomes PASS regardless of how well the community layer grows.
