# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **Stage 1 is experience-first, which is exactly what this lens asks for.** A hand-curated, complete weekly feed of Tirana events on Instagram/WhatsApp is the product experience ("everything happening, one place, trustworthy") delivered before a line of code. The founders are working back from the moment of use — "I open the feed, I see everything this week, I tap" — instead of building an app and hoping. That ordering is rare and right.
- **The pitch passes the one-sentence test.** "Everything happening in Tirana, one tap to join" is clear, and the verified research confirms the promise is currently kept by nobody: the best alternatives are a polluted auto-aggregator (AllEvents), a reportedly dead government app (TEA), and a thin competitor (Even8, ~37 events). Completeness-plus-curation is a quality difference a user feels in the first five minutes.
- **The gates are a discipline of saying no.** Each stage blocks the next, revenue is explicitly deferred, and the MVP is cut to three actions (browse, join, buy). "Everything else deferred until week-two retention is proven" is the lens's own language.
- **Curation is a craft moat the incumbents visibly lack.** The research shows the competition fails on exactly the detail work — duplicates, mislocated listings, staleness. A product that wins on completeness and accuracy wins on taste, and that is cheap to be better at and hard for an aggregator to fake.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **It is two products wearing one pitch, and the brief refuses to choose.** A community social product (create a chess night, meet strangers) and a ticketing/promotion platform (commission, sponsored placement, organizer analytics) are different experiences for different users with different failure modes. The lens says focus means saying no; this brief says "both, sequenced." Sequencing is not choosing — the commercial layer's requirements (payments, organizer tools, placement that doesn't pollute the feed the free users came for) will leak backward into the community product's design from day one. Sponsored placement at the top of the feed is a direct tax on the one thing the product must do brilliantly: show me what's actually worth going to. The moment the feed's first slot is sold, the curation promise is compromised.
2. **The experience is not ownable at its most important seams.** The first five minutes of a *community* event is not in the app — it is a stranger walking up to a yoga group by the lake. If three people show up to a twelve-person event, or the organizer flakes, the platform ate the blame and the user never returns; nothing in the brief says who owns that moment. On the commercial side the seam is worse: at 8.6% online-ticketing penetration, the buy-and-enter experience runs through cash habits, agents, and venue doors the platform does not control. EasyPay's moat is literally a nationwide cash-agent network — i.e., owning the seam this product can't. A product lens reading: the parts of the journey where this breaks are precisely the parts the founders don't touch.
3. **Craft requires presence, and the team has one part-time founder in the city, sometimes.** Hand-curating a complete weekly feed, personally seeding and supporting free events, onboarding organizers by hand — the brief's own plan is daily, physical, editorial labor in Tirana. Three part-timers in Milan/Tirana, Barcelona and London, with full-time commitment "not yet decided," cannot do detail work at the standard the pitch requires. The research shows quality-of-detail is the whole differentiation; a team structurally unable to sweat details will ship the same stale, polluted feed it is trying to replace.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. What is the one thing? If forced to cut either the community layer or ticketing from the next twelve months entirely, which goes — and why isn't it already cut from the brief?
2. Walk me through a new user's first five minutes on a wet Tuesday in February, when the real event count is low. What do they see, and why do they come back Wednesday?
3. A user one-taps into a book club and it's awkward, half-empty, or cancelled. Who owns that moment? What does the product do about a bad event it recommended?
4. Who, by name, is physically in Tirana every week doing the curation and showing up at seeded events — and when does that person go full-time? (The commitment decision is listed as open; for this plan it is not optional.)
5. When you sell the first sponsored placement, what rule protects the feed's integrity — and would you turn down a paying promoter whose event is bad?
6. At 8.6% online-ticketing penetration, what does "buy a ticket" actually look like end to end, including cash buyers and the venue door? Which parts do you control?

Open questions the pack must answer (no numbers invented here): follower-to-attendee conversion and whether mid-tier organizers will route sales through a new platform → /founder-consumer (on attendees and on organizers); commission rate and placement price the market bears → /founder-pricing; cash required to sustain the free layer until the commercial layer pays, and runway → /founder-cfo.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Name the one thing in writing: community event discovery is the product; ticketing is a feature added only after week-two retention is proven.** No organizer-facing build, no commission plumbing, before that gate.
2. **One named founder full-time and resident in Tirana before Stage 2 begins.** Hand-curation and event seeding from three time zones is not a plan.
3. **Stage 1 gate made numeric before it starts** (a stated follower count and a verified follower-to-attendee conversion threshold, set with /founder-consumer), with a pre-agreed stop decision if missed — the brief promises gates; hold them to specific ones.
4. **A written feed-integrity rule before the first placement is sold:** how many sponsored slots, how labeled, and the right to refuse — so the revenue layer cannot cannibalize the experience that creates the audience.
5. **Define and instrument the offline moment:** a minimum bar for seeded events (confirmed host, minimum attendance handling, follow-up), because the event itself is the product the app merely points at.
