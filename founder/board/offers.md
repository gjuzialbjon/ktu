# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The buyers are easy to find and reach.** The Offers lens scores a market on pain, money, reachability and growth. Reachability is the one clear pass here: the paying customers (bars, clubs, venues, independent organizers in one city) are a finite, visible list you could knock on door by door. The founders already plan 10–15 seed partnerships with assisted onboarding — that is exactly the right move, because it attacks the effort term of the value equation for the hardest-to-win side.
- **The attendee side of the offer is genuinely low-friction.** Free app, one place to look, RSVP in a tap. On the value equation, the attendee offer scores well on effort and time. That matters because the attendee base *is* the product being sold to organizers.
- **The ratings system is a real stack item.** "Know before you go" (and for organizers: "proof your event was good") is a solution to a named buyer problem that neither Instagram nor Facebook provides in structured form, and it is cheap to deliver. Informal feedback called it "very useful" — the only element of the concept with a specific positive signal attached.
- **Analytics for organizers is a second real stack item.** Organizers promoting on Instagram today get vanity metrics; "X people saw it, Y registered, Z showed up" is measurable value the current channels do not give a small Tirana organizer.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The offer to the paying customer is an audience that does not exist yet.** Strip the freemium language and the actual product sold is: "pay us for promotion, visibility and analytics" — i.e., pay for reach. On launch day the app's reach is zero. The value equation collapses on *perceived likelihood*: why would an organizer believe paying this app fills a room better than a free Instagram post to followers they already have? The founders name the chicken-and-egg risk themselves, but it is sharper than they state it: it is not a growth problem, it is a hole in the middle of the offer. Until there is a user base, the organizer offer has no believable mechanism of delivering its dream outcome, and the brief says first revenue is expected only 6–9 months after launch — the *time* term of the value equation is terrible too.

2. **The market may fail the "buyers have money" test.** The Offers lens says pick the market first; the most common reason this lens passes is a weak market. The money side here is untested: break-even needs ~100–150 active paying clients in one city whose venues are described as needing prices "affordable for the Tirana market." Nobody knows what a Tirana bar currently spends on event promotion, whether that budget exists at all beyond free Instagram posts, or what the total pool of organizers who would ever pay is. If the answer is "most of them spend €0 and boost a post occasionally," there is no market, however good the app. Open questions, no inventing numbers: current organizer promotion spend and the real size of the paying-organizer pool → **/founder-competitors**; willingness to pay and at what price → **/founder-consumer**; what price × 100–150 clients must equal to cover costs → **/founder-cfo**.

3. **The differentiation is convenience against free incumbents — a price-shaped fight.** The stated edge is "complete and centralized," and the stated pricing posture is "affordable." The lens is blunt about this: if the main difference is being cheaper and tidier than Instagram/Facebook (which cost organizers nothing and already hold the audience), this is a commodity offer. The test the lens applies — *could you double the price and still be the obvious choice?* — fails today, because there is no guarantee, no proof, no promised outcome, only features. Nothing in the brief would make an organizer feel stupid saying no. The informal "generally positive" conversations are not proof; people say yes to free hypotheticals.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. What is the organizer's dream outcome in one sentence, and what number do you promise? "Visibility" is not an outcome; "30 extra paying attendees at your Friday event" is.
2. What does a Tirana bar or independent organizer spend today, in lek per month, to promote events — and on what? (If you cannot answer, run **/founder-competitors** on the promotion channels, not just the event apps.)
3. Why would organizer #16 — the first one you *don't* hand-hold — believe this works? What proof asset exists at that moment?
4. What guarantee can you afford to attach to the first paid packages? ("If your event gets fewer than N registrations through us, the promotion is free" — can the economics carry that? → **/founder-cfo**.)
5. Will attendees actually switch a discovery habit from Instagram for this, and what buy/usage rate should we assume? (→ **/founder-consumer**, run on both sides: attendees and organizers.)
6. Why is ticketing — the one revenue line where you take a cut of money that demonstrably already flows — deferred to "later," while the unproven promotion-package revenue is first?
7. Three part-time founders and a 3–4 month MVP: who is doing the 10–15 partnership sales, which is the part of this plan that is actually an offer being sold?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Pre-sell before you build.** Get at least 10 of the 10–15 seed organizers to commit *money* (even a token pre-paid package or signed letter of intent with a price on it) before the MVP is funded. Informal positive conversations do not count; a price accepted does. This single test answers the "buyers have money" question no research can.
2. **Name the price and the promise.** A concrete organizer package: price in lek, a stated outcome, and a guarantee the numbers can survive. Run **/founder-cfo** to confirm that 100–150 clients at that price actually reaches break-even with the guarantee's cost included, and **/founder-consumer** on organizers at that price point.
3. **Reorder the offer around proof.** The first paid product should be the one with built-in proof of delivery (attendance analytics on seeded events, or ticketing commission where payment only happens when value is delivered), not visibility promises. Promotion packages come after there is an audience to sell.
4. **Close the market-size hole.** **/founder-competitors** to establish how many Tirana organizers pay anything for promotion today and what Kuadr Events, Enter Event and Go Wild charge (if anything) — the brief says they exist and are small, but not why they stayed small. If the answer is "no budget exists in this market," revert this vote to PASS.
