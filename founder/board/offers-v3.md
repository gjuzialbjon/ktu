# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The commission structure is the right shape of offer.** A percentage of tickets already being sold is money that already flows; the organizer risks nothing up front and pays only on results. The Offers lens favours exactly this: an offer where the buyer's downside is near zero and payment tracks the outcome. It is the closest thing in this brief to "stupid to say no."
- **The staging refuses to sell exposure that does not exist.** Gating revenue behind demonstrated reach (stage 3 requires signed commercial commitments or stop) directly addresses the weakest point of most offers — perceived likelihood. The founders are at least trying to manufacture proof before asking for money.
- **The free side of the value equation is genuinely good for attendees.** One tap to join, zero price, zero commission ever: low effort, low sacrifice, clear outcome (a social life beyond cafés). As an audience-building asset, the free layer asks almost nothing of its users.
- **Stage 1 costs nothing and produces the one proof the paid offer needs.** Verifiable follower-to-attendee conversion is exactly the evidence a sponsor or promoter would need to believe the placement works.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The paying market may be too small and too poor to matter.** The Offers lens says pick the market first: massive pain, money to spend, easy to reach, growing. The brief can only confirm "easy to reach." How many Tirana events are ticketed, at what prices, and what promoters actually spend on promotion is explicitly unknown. If commercial organizers in a 500k-person city spend little on promotion and move modest ticket volume, a "small percentage per ticket" of a small flow is a rounding error, and no offer engineering fixes a market with no money in it. Open question — /founder-competitors (incumbent pricing, ticketed-event volume, why incumbents stalled) and /founder-cfo (what revenue the flows could actually support).
2. **At the moment of sale, likelihood is zero.** Stage 3 asks promoters to sign commission terms and sponsors to commit to placement pricing *before the app exists* — the offer is sold precisely when the platform has no proof it can deliver attention or ticket sales. The value equation says offers die on likelihood, and willingness of organizers to route ticket sales through a new platform is listed as unknown. "Signed commitments" obtained against zero proof are likely to be soft LOIs that evaporate at launch. Open question — /founder-consumer run on organizers, not attendees.
3. **On the attention side it competes with free, and the offer has no stack to beat it.** Instagram already holds the audience at zero cost to the promoter; the local platforms already court the same commercial buyers. The brief's paid offer today is bare: a commission (rate unset), a feed placement (price unset), no guarantee, no bonuses, no real urgency, no named package. Nothing yet answers the lens's test — what makes a promoter feel stupid saying no instead of posting on Instagram as they do now? Until the audience exists AND the offer is stacked, this is a commodity ad product priced against free. Pricing structure — /founder-pricing; stack construction — /founder-offer once organizer pain is documented.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. How many ticketed events happen in Tirana per year, at what average ticket price and volume, and what does a typical promoter spend per event on promotion? (Nobody has these numbers yet — /founder-competitors.)
2. What exactly counts as a "signed commitment" at stage 3 — money escrowed, a rate card signed, or a friendly LOI? And what is the stated minimum number before you proceed? (/founder-consumer on organizers to test what they will actually sign.)
3. What is the guarantee? E.g., for the first sponsors: "front of feed for a month; if we do not deliver X verified attendees/clicks, you pay nothing." Can the economics afford one? (/founder-cfo.)
4. Who is the single beachhead paying buyer — a concert promoter, a venue, a festival — and what is their current cost per attendee acquired through Instagram ads and posters? Could you double your eventual price against that number and still be the obvious choice? (/founder-pricing.)
5. What is the numeric gate for stage 1 (followers, and what follower-to-attendee conversion rate passes), decided now, before the data arrives?
6. What stops a promoter from using the feed for free discovery while keeping ticket sales in their existing channel — i.e., what is bundled into the ticketing offer that they cannot get by free-riding?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Market size proven before any build.** /founder-competitors delivers verified counts of ticketed events, ticket volumes and current promoter promotion spend in Tirana, with sources. If annual addressable promotion-plus-commission spend cannot plausibly support the business, stop — per the lens, no offer survives a market without money.
2. **Stage 1 gate written down in advance.** A specific follower count and a specific, verifiable follower-to-attendee conversion rate, committed before the feed launches, with "miss means stop or rework."
3. **Stage 3 commitments must be hard.** A defined minimum of commercial organizers signed to actual commission rates and sponsors to actual placement prices — terms on paper with numbers, not expressions of interest — validated by /founder-consumer run on organizers and priced by /founder-pricing.
4. **A real paid offer built before money is asked for.** At minimum a performance guarantee for the first cohort of sponsors and a named launch package with honest scarcity (e.g., a capped number of founding-sponsor slots), affordability confirmed by /founder-cfo.
5. **Unit economics re-derived.** /founder-cfo rebuilds break-even from the real commission rate and sponsorship price once they exist; the brief itself voids the earlier numbers.
