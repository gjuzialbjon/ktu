# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The plan knows how to say no.** Stage 4 explicitly cuts ratings, notifications, user-created events and image-heavy profiles from v1, and each comes back only after week-two retention of the core loop is proven. That is the lens's first demand — focus by subtraction — applied before a line of code exists. Most briefs fail here; this one doesn't.
- **Stage 1 is the experience, not a proxy for it.** A hand-curated, genuinely complete weekly feed on channels Tirana already uses is the product's core experience delivered at zero build cost. If people won't consume it free, nothing downstream matters, and the plan says so.
- **Hands-on craft at the wedge.** Founders personally onboarding organizers and attending events to control quality is the right instinct: at the start, the event itself is a touchpoint of the product, and the only way to keep it from embarrassing you is to be in the room.
- **The kill gates are real.** "0 organizers sign → stop, nothing spent" is a plan that can actually fail cheaply, which is rarer than it sounds.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **It is two products wearing one pitch.** The one-liner itself carries the contradiction: "every event in Tirana" (a completeness product — exhaustive coverage, big venues included, curation as a treadmill of founder hours forever) versus "starting with the small ones nobody else lists" (a niche community product — depth, trust, personal onboarding). These demand different experiences, different feeds, different success metrics. Worse, the gates don't connect: stage 1 validates the completeness feed, then stage 3 sells the niche wedge. A few thousand followers of a complete feed is not evidence that a yoga teacher will pay. The lens asks "what is the one thing?" and this brief has not answered it.
2. **The money moment and the wedge never meet, and the promised experience can't be delivered where it's sold.** The guarantee that gates stage 3 — "30 extra attendees at your event, or it's free" — is sold before the platform has any mechanism to deliver attendees beyond an Instagram account's reach, and the brief gives no evidence that reach converts to feet in a room. Meanwhile the first revenue line is ticketing commission — "a cut of money that already demonstrably flows" — but at yoga by the lake, book clubs and group hikes, money mostly does not flow; the events that sell tickets are exactly the commercial ones the wedge concedes to incumbents. The second line, attendance analytics ("Z showed up"), requires verifying physical attendance at informal events — an unbuilt check-in experience the brief never mentions. Owning the whole experience means the promise, the delivery and the receipt all have to work; here each one has a seam.
3. **The hardest handoff in the plan is unexamined: feed → app.** If stage 1 succeeds, the product that succeeded is an Instagram/WhatsApp feed — zero friction, already installed, already in the user's daily loop. Stage 4 then asks those followers to download an app whose only addition is a one-tap RSVP. The lens starts from the experience and works back: what is the moment where the app is clearly better than the feed that already works? The brief never describes it. Users who cross platform seams need a reason measured in their time, not the founders' roadmap; without one, stage 1's success becomes stage 4's ceiling. (That three local platforms built apps and stayed small, while Instagram kept the audience, is consistent with this seam being the killer — why they stalled is the open question /founder-competitors must answer before stage 4 is approved.)

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. What is the one thing — the complete feed or the informal-events niche? If forced to cut one of the two halves of your own pitch, which goes?
2. Describe, minute by minute, a first-time user's first five minutes in the stage-4 app — and then the same five minutes on the Instagram feed. What is different, and would the user notice?
3. Walk me through the evening of a seeded event: how do you know "Z showed up"? Who checks people in at a lakeside yoga class, with what, and what happens when they don't bother?
4. When you promise an organizer "30 extra attendees or it's free," what specifically produces those attendees at the moment you first sell it, and what did it cost to produce them? (Nobody has this number — /founder-consumer should test whether organizers believe the promise and what proof they need; /founder-pricing should set what the promise is worth.)
5. Informal events are largely free to attend. What does the organizer of a free book club ever pay you for, and how much? (Open question; /founder-consumer for willingness, /founder-cfo to re-derive the 100–150-client break-even once real prices exist.)
6. Why did Kuadr Events, Enter Event and Go Wild stall — product, distribution, or the market itself? (Open question, flagged in the brief; /founder-competitors.)
7. Hand curation of "every event in Tirana" is the product's quality bar. How many founder-hours a week does completeness cost at stage 1, and what keeps that bar when the full-time founder is also building the app?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Resolve the two-product contradiction before stage 2.** One written sentence naming the single product, and stage gates re-cut so that each gate validates the thing the next stage builds — specifically, stage 1 must measure engagement with the *informal-events* portion of the feed separately, since that is what stage 3 sells.
2. **An experience spec before any stage-4 code:** the first five minutes of the app, written from the user's side, including the stated reason it beats the free feed the same users already follow. If no honest reason exists, stage 4 is cancelled and the business is re-scoped around the feed.
3. **Guarantee only what is deliverable.** The stage-3 promise must name its delivery mechanism (what produces the 30 attendees, at what cost per attendee) before it is sold, or be rewritten to a promise the platform can keep.
4. **A working attendance-verification design** (even a manual one) before any analytics revenue is promised to an organizer.
5. **/founder-competitors on why the three local platforms stayed small, delivered before stage 4 is greenlit** — if the answer is "apps lose to Instagram in this market," that is a product verdict, not a marketing problem.
