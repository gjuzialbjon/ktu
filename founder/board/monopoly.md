# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong

- **The market shape is right.** The Monopoly lens says start with a big share of a small market, and "all events in Tirana" is a genuinely small, bounded market: one city, ~500k residents, dozens of events a week, hundreds of organizers. That is the kind of market one player *could* own before expanding to other Albanian cities. The founders are not making the classic mistake of pitching "the events app for the Balkans" on day one.
- **The moat type is the right one.** Of the four durable advantages the lens names (proprietary tech, network effects, scale economies, brand), this business is built around the strongest: network effects. A two-sided events marketplace that reaches liquidity in one city is genuinely hard to displace — attendees go where the events are, organizers go where the attendees are. If the flywheel ever spins, it defends itself.
- **Honest self-diagnosis.** The founders name the chicken-and-egg problem themselves and have a concrete seeding plan (10–15 organizer partnerships with assisted onboarding, organic before paid). The lens says distribution is half the business; at least they are thinking about it rather than assuming "build it and they will come."
- **A real fragmentation gap exists.** Three local platforms each cover "only a small percentage of total events" — nobody has won this market. The monopoly seat is empty.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **There is no secret.** The lens asks: what does the founder know that others do not? "Event information in Tirana is fragmented" is not a secret — it is visible to everyone, including the three local platforms already acting on it (Kuadr Events, Enter Event, Go Wild) and to every organizer who defaults to Instagram. The empty monopoly seat may be empty because the market does not sustain a dedicated platform: organizers already reach their audience free on Instagram/Facebook, where the audience already is. Nothing in the brief is a 10x improvement over "post the event on Instagram" — structured search and ratings are incremental, and the lens is explicit that incremental improvements do not create monopolies. Why three local platforms stalled is the single most important unknown, and the brief does not answer it. (Open question for /founder-competitors: what exactly killed or capped the three incumbents — supply, demand, or monetization?)
2. **Nothing stops a copycat in month six — including the incumbents.** The planned product (create, discover, RSVP, notify, rate) is a standard feature set on a commodity stack (React Native/Flutter + Firebase); any of the three local platforms, or a venue group, could replicate it in a season. Until network density exists, the moat is a plan for a moat, not a moat. And the team most able to defend it is three part-time founders in Tirana, Barcelona and London — against the lens's people question, a network-effects land-grab run by nobody full-time, from two other countries, is close to disqualifying. Monopolies in winner-take-all markets go to whoever seeds supply fastest; part-time loses that race by default.
3. **Distribution is asserted, not evidenced — and the paying side may be too small to matter.** The lens says a good product with no way to reach customers is not a business. First 100 users: unproven (no waitlist, no pre-sales, no CAC estimate — only informal conversations). Worse, the monopoly being chased may be profitless: break-even needs 100–150 paying organizers out of "hundreds" in the whole city — a majority-share requirement in the *paying* market before pricing has even been tested, in a market described as needing "affordable" prices. Dominating a small market only works, per the lens, if the small market can pay for the dominance. (Open questions: willingness-to-pay and price points → /founder-consumer then /founder-pricing; CAC per side and seeding cost → /founder-cfo; real organizer counts and incumbent pricing → /founder-competitors.)

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. What do you know about Tirana's events market that Kuadr Events, Enter Event and Go Wild did not? Be specific — why did they stall, and why does that reason not apply to you?
2. Why now? What changed in Tirana in the last two years that makes this possible today when it was not before? "The problem exists" is not a timing answer.
3. What is the smaller-than-Tirana first market — one neighbourhood, one scene (e.g. fitness and outdoor meetups), one university — where you can reach event liquidity with 10–15 organizers, and what does "liquidity" mean in numbers?
4. If this starts working, Instagram costs an organizer nothing and already holds their followers. What is the concrete event-day experience that is 10x better than an Instagram story plus a WhatsApp group — not 20% better?
5. Who goes full-time, when, and what triggers it? A network-effects race cannot be won on evenings and weekends from three countries.
6. The 100–150 paying clients at break-even — what share of Tirana's total addressable organizers is that, and what evidence do you have that any of them will pay anything? One signed letter of intent would outweigh every informal conversation in this brief.
7. What stops Go Wild from copying your creation-plus-ratings feature set in month six, before you have density?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

Not applicable — the vote is PASS. For completeness, the findings that would reopen it: a stated secret about why the incumbents failed, validated with /founder-competitors; evidence of organizer willingness to pay (pre-sales or signed LOIs, not conversations — /founder-consumer and /founder-pricing); a wedge market smaller than all-of-Tirana with a numeric liquidity target; and at least one founder full-time at launch. This is a PASS on the business as briefed, not on the category — the small-market, network-effects shape is correct; the secret, the team commitment and the proof of a paying side are missing.
