# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The staged rollout is built the way the Product lens says to build: experience first, infrastructure second.** Stage 1 is the customer moment itself — a complete, curated feed someone actually follows and acts on — delivered with zero code. The brief works back from "everything happening in Tirana, one tap to join" to what must be true to deliver it, and refuses to build the app until the experience is proven on borrowed rails. That is the right order.
- **The pitch passes the one-sentence test.** "Everything happening in Tirana, one tap to join — free for the community, and the big events pay to reach it" is clear, and the free/paid split is legible to both sides without a paragraph of explanation.
- **The gates are a discipline of saying no.** No revenue before reach; no app before signed commercial commitments; no ratings or notifications before week-two retention. A stated "or stop" condition on stage 3 is rare in briefs and is exactly the cut-until-essential posture the lens asks for.
- **Founders attending and hand-onboarding the small events (stage 2) is owning the experience at the only moment that matters early** — the event itself, not the listing.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The product is asked to do three things brilliantly, and the brief has not chosen one.** A complete discovery feed, a social tool for creating/joining community events, and a ticketing + advertising business are three products with different failure modes. The lens's first question — what is the one thing? — has no answer here. "Complete feed" is the implied one thing, but completeness is a treadmill: the week the feed misses a big concert or the lake yoga session, the promise "everything happening in Tirana" is broken, and that promise *is* the product. Three part-time founders, one of them only "regularly" in Tirana, cannot hand-curate completeness and hand-support community liquidity and close commercial deals simultaneously. Something will be done badly, and in this product, badly done curation is visible to every user every day.
2. **The commercial layer is a seam that cuts through the soul of the product.** Sponsored placement means the top of the feed — the first five seconds of every session — is sold to the highest payer the moment revenue starts. The free community layer is pitched as the soul; the ad model monetizes exactly the surface where that soul lives. The brief contains no design answer for how a paid concert sits above a free book club without the feed starting to feel like the Instagram clutter it claims to replace. Seams are where products break, and this seam is at the center of the screen.
3. **Ticketing hands the worst moments of the experience to people the platform does not control.** The buyer's touchpoints run: discover → pay (payments in Albania: method, failure, refund policy — none specified) → receive ticket → be scanned or turned away at a door staffed by the organizer. If the organizer's door staff mishandles a platform ticket, the customer blames the platform. The brief never describes the ticket-buying and door moment, which is precisely the moment the lens says to design first. Until that handoff is owned end to end, the revenue layer is a liability wearing a business model. The same applies one layer down: a dismal community event found through the platform is, to the attendee, a dismal platform.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. What is the one thing? If you could ship only the feed, only the community join-flow, or only ticketing, which do you keep — and why isn't the brief already just that?
2. Walk me through the first five minutes: a 26-year-old in Tirana opens the app on a Tuesday evening. What do they see, what do they tap, and what happens at the event door? Now the same for a promoter listing a ticketed concert.
3. Who curates the feed to completeness every single week, in person, in Tirana — by name — and what happens the week they are in Milan, Barcelona or London? (Full-time commitment is listed as undecided; for this product, curation quality *is* the product.)
4. What is the design rule for sponsored placement — how many paid slots, labeled how, and what will you refuse to sell — so the community feed does not become the clutter you are replacing?
5. What exactly happens when a ticket purchase fails, an event is cancelled, or a buyer is refused at the door? Who refunds, on what rail, within what time?
6. Why did Kuadr Events, Enter Event and Go Wild stall — was it a product failure you can out-craft, or a market fact that will stall you too? (Open question; the brief says it's unverified — /founder-competitors.)
7. Will commercial organizers actually route ticket sales and promotion spend through you, and at what commission/placement price? Nobody has these numbers — /founder-consumer (run on organizers) and /founder-pricing.
8. What does a user cost to acquire and what budget/runway carries you through stages 1–4? Not in the brief — /founder-cfo.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Stage 1 gate made numeric before it starts, and enforced.** Specific follower and verified follower-to-attendee conversion thresholds written down now; miss them and the answer is stop, as the brief itself promises. (Thresholds to be sanity-checked against /founder-consumer output.)
2. **One thing declared in writing.** Before any code: a single sentence naming the one capability the MVP must do brilliantly, and a list of what is cut (the lens predicts ratings, notifications, analytics, promotion packages — the brief already defers these; hold that line).
3. **At least one founder full-time and physically in Tirana before stage 2 begins.** Hand-curation and hand-onboarding cannot be done part-time from three countries; the brief leaves this undecided, which for this product is a product defect, not an HR detail.
4. **The ticket experience specified end to end before stage 3 commitments are signed:** payment method(s) available to Tirana buyers, refund policy, cancellation flow, and the door/validation moment — including who answers the support message at 22:00 on a Friday.
5. **A written sponsored-placement policy** (slot count, labeling, refusal criteria) that protects the community feed, agreed before the first placement is sold.
6. **Stage 3 "or stop" honored with a number:** the minimum count and value of signed ticketing and sponsorship commitments defined in advance, with pricing grounded in /founder-pricing and the economics re-derived by /founder-cfo — the brief correctly voids all earlier break-even math.
